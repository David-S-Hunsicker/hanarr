Hanarr runs entirely on your machine: it parses your resume, searches job sources with legitimate public APIs, scores how well each posting fits your stated preferences, and reminds you to follow up. You review matches and make the calls — this page is a short map of how the pieces fit together, not a full manual.

Recommended order of operations

  1. Set up a local AI model (Settings → App config) — Hanarr is built around an LLM actually reading your resume and each posting, not keyword matching; do this before step 2 so your resume is parsed properly the first time instead of needing a later retry. Free, runs entirely on your machine (Ollama), one explicit click to install.
  2. Settings — add your resume and set your preferences (target titles, locations, salary floor, dealbreakers, which job sources to enable). This is the only step you have to do before anything else is useful. Without a model from step 1, resume parsing and job scoring both fall back to plain keyword matching — a much blunter signal than an LLM actually reading the text, and the degraded mode, not the intended one.
  3. Jobs — review what was found, sorted by fit. Mark status as you apply, hear back, or interview; that automatically schedules a follow-up reminder.
  4. Coaching & Skills — when a job shows a skill gap, decide whether to close it with a quick refresher or a small project, and track your real capability evidence separately from what your resume happens to say.
  5. Applications — your pipeline by status, once you've started acting on matches.
  6. Prep — once an interview is actually on the calendar, practice behavioral questions built from your real work history and write them up as STAR stories ahead of time.

What each page is for

Jobs

Discovery and decisions. Every matched posting, its fit score, and why it scored that way. "Improve my fit" starts a coaching project for a posting's specific gaps without changing the score itself — a score only changes when you approve a resume update from a completed project.

Coaching

Active skill-gap projects: what to work on, why, and a review-first way to submit evidence (written response, local files, or a GitHub repository) for feedback before it ever touches your resume. A passed evaluation completes the project automatically and offers a resume proposal; you can also mark a project complete yourself (for work you're satisfied with without submitting it for review) or cancel one you're abandoning. A project with no activity in two weeks is flagged stale as a nudge, not a deadline.

Resume

Your actual source content, the profile Hanarr extracted from it, and any pending resume changes proposed by a completed project — nothing is applied without your explicit approval, and every version stays available to roll back to.

Skills

Capability, evidence, and resume wording, kept deliberately separate: a skill mentioned on your resume is not the same as one with real project evidence behind it, and this page shows you which is which. "Quick skill check" tests a claimed skill with a short Q&A instead of just trusting the resume wording or a self-reported number, and gives you an explicit next step (a refresher, a retake, or a coaching project) based on the answer.

Applications

The jobs you've actually acted on, viewed as a pipeline by status, with any pending reminders.

Prep

Behavioral ("tell me about a time you...") interview practice. Generates candidate questions from your actual work history, grouped by competency, and helps you write each one up as a STAR (Situation/Task/Action/Result) story — reviewing your wording and flagging a vague component or an unmeasured result, never inventing an achievement you didn't state.

Settings

Preferences and matching criteria, job sources, your LLM provider, resume upload, scheduling, and update checks — everything that controls how Hanarr behaves. "Per-task model sizing" lets you give a specific task (like fit scoring) a differently sized model than the rest, if you're running a local model and want to tune the tradeoff yourself.

Understanding fit scores and skill gaps

A fit score reflects how well a posting matches your stated preferences and resume — it's a starting signal for triage, not a verdict. Click "View fit analysis" on a job to see exactly which skills it's looking for and how each one is classified: satisfied (your profile clearly covers it), partial (some evidence, but thinner than the posting wants), or missing. "Improve my fit" turns a missing or partial skill into a coaching project; it reappears automatically if your active resume changes after an analysis, so a stale result never silently goes unnoticed.

Local AI vs. a hosted model

The default provider is Ollama, running fully on your own machine — free, private, no API key, but it needs to actually be installed and have a model downloaded before scoring quality is good (Settings will tell you exactly what's missing and walk you through it, one explicit click at a time; nothing downloads without your consent). Switching to Anthropic in Settings uses a hosted Claude model instead — higher quality, but your resume text and job descriptions are sent to Anthropic's API, and it incurs usage cost. Skipping both (provider "none") still works, just with plain keyword-overlap scoring instead of an LLM actually reading each posting.

A few things worth knowing

  • Preferences fields on Settings autosave as you edit them — there's still a Save button, but you don't have to remember to click it.
  • More than one person can use the same instance: the Profiles page lets you switch which local profile this browser is acting as — each gets its own resume, jobs, applications, and skills, while search preferences stay shared.
  • Nothing Hanarr does is automatic in a way that could surprise you: projects are opted into, resume changes are proposals requiring your approval, and application submission is never unattended.
